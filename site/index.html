How it works

From a contributor's upload to an agent's training run

Every step is automated. People supply the data; software checks it, grows it, signs it and delivers it.

  1. The SourceContributors pass an ID check, sign an ownership agreement and upload.
  2. The AssayEight layers of checks: origin, file forensics, personal-data scrub, quality.
  3. The ForgeVerified seeds are expanded into synthetic records. Every prompt is logged.
  4. Pit BossThree independent AI judges audit a random sample. Over 5% errors and the batch is regenerated.
  5. The LedgerA manifest of sources, consents, licenses, prompts and hashes is signed and published.
  6. The DeckPackaged, graded and listed in the catalog with a free sample.
  7. The TableAgents search, read manifests, order custom sets and buy through the API or MCP.
  8. The CageChecks the budget cap, charges the account and pays contributor royalties.
  9. The ShoeDelivers a signed download or a direct share to S3, Snowflake or Hugging Face.

Verification, plainly

What we check, and what we can't promise

No one can prove with certainty that an uploader owns their data. We stack checks that catch most problems, put legal responsibility on the contributor, and grade every Deck by how much was checked.

A real-world order

How a pump-repair AI got its training data

A startup builds an agent that helps factory technicians diagnose pump failures. It needs 5,000 troubleshooting Q&As and can't legally scrape forums.

Illustrative example. Numbers show how an order would flow, not a real customer.

  1. Request

    The agent finds no matching Deck and calls request_dataset with its spec and a $2,000 budget.

  2. Quote

    A $1,800 quote comes back. It's inside the account's spending rules, so the order starts automatically.

  3. Seeds

    A bounty goes out to 25 vetted technicians, who write 300 Q&As from experience at $3 per accepted item.

  4. Forge

    Seeds pass the Assay and are expanded to 5,000 records, each with its generating prompt.

  5. Audit

    Filters drop 400 weak records. The judge panel audits 200 at random, and the batch passes.

  6. Delivery

    Parquet files, a datasheet and a signed manifest land in the buyer's S3 bucket.

  7. Royalties

    The Deck joins the catalog. Every resale pays the 25 contributors again.

Questions

Is anyone reviewing data by hand?

No. Verification, auditing, pricing, purchasing and delivery are fully automated. People are involved only as contributors who supply the original data.

Is Data Dealer a RAG system?

No. RAG is a way for an AI to look things up while it answers. Data Dealer is a marketplace and data supply chain. Many buyers use our Decks to build their own RAG systems.

Why sell synthetic data at all?

Real expert data is scarce and expensive. Synthetic data grown from verified real seeds gives buyers scale, and every synthetic record is labeled with the prompt that made it so they can decide how to use it.

What happens if bad data gets through?

Every Deck can be taken down, the contributor's payout clawed back, and buyers notified with a corrected version.

Can my agent buy without me?

Yes, within the budget cap and per-purchase limit you set when you create the account.
